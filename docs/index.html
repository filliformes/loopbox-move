16-track stereo tape looper · Ableton Move

LoopBox

A looping instrument for ambient and experimental music. Sixteen free-running tape loops, four playheads each, a deep set of performance effects, and every gesture click-free.

16 stereo loops 4 playheads / loop 16 punch-in FX 29 send effects 4 reverbs 32 session slots Schwung Overtake

Built for the Schwung framework on Ableton Move. Free & open source: source on GitHub.

The idea

A looper you play like an instrument

LoopBox turns Move's pads into sixteen independent tape loops that never have to agree on a length or a downbeat. You layer sound freely, then treat every loop as raw material: re-pitched, re-ordered, filtered, granulated, frozen, with the whole surface live under your hands.

Free-running

Each loop is its own tape. Record one at 3 seconds and another at 40, in different keys, at different speeds. Nothing is quantized to a grid unless you ask.

Everything performs

Speed, pitch, filter, grain, reverb sends, playheads: all live per loop. Sixteen momentary punch-in effects sit on the right pads, pressure-sensitive and stackable.

Click-free

Every jump, mute, overdub and knob move is crossfaded or smoothed. You can be violent with it and it stays musical.

LoopBox draws its screen and widgets with Schwung's own param-page renderer, so it looks and behaves like a native Move instrument: round knobs, notched enum squares, animated buttons, header and footer.
Quickstart

Your first loop in a minute

  1. Open LoopBox from the Overtake / Tools list in Schwung. The left 4×4 pads are your loops; the right 4×4 are punch-in effects.
  2. Record. Tap an empty left pad to start recording, tap again to close the loop and play. It loops instantly.
  3. Layer. Tap other empty pads and record more loops on top. They run independently: no need to match lengths.
  4. Shape it. Press a step button to select a loop and see its waveform. Turn the eight knobs to sweep Filter, Pitch, Scatter, Seed and more across its four pages (Up/Down change page).
  5. Punch an effect. Hold a right pad: Haze, Freeze, Chop: over the whole mix. Press harder for more. Shift+pad latches it hands-free.
  6. Send to a reverb. On loop page 1, turn Send A up; open Send FX (track button 3) and choose Veil or Space. Then Save it all to a session with the record button.

Full-exit is Shift+Back. A plain Back only suspends, keeping your loops in memory.

The screen

What you see

Three views. A touched knob takes over the header with the parameter's full name and value; the views fall back to the overview after ten seconds.

Input

What goes onto the tape

Everything you record passes through the input stage first, so set it before you play. The Input FX menu (track button 1) holds the monitor level, the tape style, input gain and a three-band EQ. The Input Tape page (Capture button) is the machine itself: 13 styles including a true Tapeless bypass, drive, wow, flutter, HF rolloff, low cut, hiss and Generations, the sound of repeated dubbing. Whatever you dial here is printed onto the loop.

Loops & playheads

Sixteen tapes, four heads each

Each loop records up to 45 seconds of stereo audio and plays back on its own clock. Three overdub modes shape how layers build: Replace, Multiply (the default; endless decayed layering), and Disintegration (the loop's own effects re-bake each pass, so it slowly falls apart).

Every loop is read by up to four playheads at once, each with a mode: Off · Fwd · Bwd · Ping · Jump: and its own speed from 0.25× to 4×. Jump leaps to a random position at random intervals, crossfaded. One recording becomes a canon, a drone, or a shifting cloud. Heads sum with equal-power normalisation so stacking never clips, and any head that jumps crossfades.

Select a loop with a step, then Up/Down to move between pages. Speed re-pitches like tape (pitch + tempo together); Pitch shifts independently, leaving tempo alone.

Seed

A seeded re-order of the loop's slices (2/4/8/16, some reversed). The knob is the seed: every position is a different, repeatable mangle. Click-free.

Scatter

Stochastic slice jumps with crossfades: from gentle stutter to full shuffle.

Filter + Reso

A DJ-style filter: low-pass left of centre, high-pass right, with resonance. Smoothed, so sweeps never step.

Sends

Two buses, the Palette engine

Each loop has two sends (A on loop page 1, B on page 2) into a pair of shared effect buses: the Palette engine, 29 effects, chosen in the Send FX menu (track button 3). Every effect has the same three knobs, Amount, Macro and Drift, so a bus can be re-voiced live without relearning it.

The buses are 100% wet: the loop's own level stays dry, the send sets how much reaches the effect. The returns join the master before the punch effects, so a punch can grab a reverb tail too.

Punch-in FX

The right pads: sixteen momentary effects

Hold a pad to apply an effect to the whole mix, reading a rolling 2-second capture of the master. Up to five stack in series, each with four parameters on knobs 5–8, and pad pressure drives a per-effect expression shown in the footer.

Loops

Loop12 · Loop16 · LoopSh · Chop. Beat-locked stutters. Chop repeats the last transient on one of 32 rhythmic patterns drawn from Signal. Pressure subdivides.

Grains

Haze · Mosaic · Smear · Strum. Granular clouds: from a shimmering haze to a grid re-sequencer to an arpeggiated cascade. Pressure adds density.

Pitch

Oct− · Oct+ · Glide · Shimmer. Delay-line pitch shifts and a band-limited feedback shimmer that climbs an octave per pass. Pressure pushes regen/glide.

Time

Stretch · Freeze · Reverse · PalFX. Overlap-add stretch and freeze (with a subtle doubler for width), reversed slices, and one whole Palette effect as a punch.

A per-effect LFO on every pad

While a pad is held, knobs 1–4 are its own LFO: Dest (which of the four fx params to modulate), Shape, Rate (0.05–20 Hz) and Depth. Eight shapes: sine, triangle, saw, ramp, square, pulse, sample-and-hold, and smoothed random: for anything from a slow drift to chaotic wobble. Knobs 5–8 stay the effect's parameters.

Shift+pad latches an effect hands-free. Shift while already holding latches it exactly as it is, pressure frozen. Undo+pad resets that pad to its defaults (the LED flashes to confirm).
FX sequencer

The ✕ button: sequencing punch effects

One shared 16-step pattern of punch effects, in the spirit of the Polyend MESS. Each step can hold up to five right pads with their knobs and pressure locked at the moment you write it, so a step keeps sounding the way it did even if you edit the pad later. Every gesture starts from the ✕ (Delete) button.

The clock runs from the Move tempo. A step retriggers its effects the way a finger would, through a short fade; a pad you are holding by hand is never cut by the sequencer. Gate below Hold releases the effects part-way through the step unless the next step is an extension. Like Last follows the previous evaluated step; Play X Skip Y counts cycles per step. The pattern and all its locks save with the session.

Perform & master

The master is a performance surface too

The Perform menu (track button 2) has two pages. Page 1 is the Stumble stochastic glitcher plus Jump and Scan. Page 2 is a master processing block:

Master filter

A low-pass with twelve analog characters ported from Fizzik: Clean, SEM, MS-20, Steiner, four ladders, Prophet, Oberheim, Diode/303, K35, Vintage. Cutoff and resonance are smoothed, so sweeps never step. Self-oscillates on the ladder voicings.

Master Clock

A Chase Bliss Mood-style pitch/rate warp with sample-rate crush. Music mode snaps to wide intervals (octaves, fifths, thirds); Free slides continuously. Place it pre- or post-punch.

Ducking pump

K7–K8: a tempo-synced sidechain pump. It ducks on every beat division and breathes back up: from a gentle swell to a hard rhythmic gate.

Tape transport

The ◀ ▶ arrows brake the whole master to a stop or wind it up to a tone, then ease back to speed on release. Fully organic.

Output character (Settings page 1)

An end-of-chain colour section: a character EQ modelling ten hardware units, a bus glue compressor, and an analog tape limiter.

Drift

A memory that never quite repeats

On the Sample button: a global drifting-delay memory station in the spirit of Soma COSMOS. Four delay lines of coprime length, each read at a slowly drifting tap, feed back through a matrix that morphs from self-feedback to a normalised Hadamard cross-mix. The loop mix feeds it, the memory recirculates, and because the lengths are coprime and every line has its own asynchronous LFO, the recombination never lands on an exact repeat. It sits in the master chain just before the pump, so its ambient layer takes the character EQ, glue and limiter. Feedback is capped below unity, so the tail always fades: up to a few minutes at the top of the range. Leave everything paused and a silence bleed clears an abandoned tail after about eight seconds with no input. Drift and Mix start at zero, so it stays silent until you dial it in, and it saves with the session.

Drift · Rate · Size

Drift is how much of the loop mix is written into the memory. Rate is how fast the read taps drift. Size sets the tap length, from a close shimmer to a long hall.

FBk · Supr

FBk is the memory sustain: it is capped below unity so the tail always fades, up to a few minutes at maximum. Supr lets loud new input erase the old content, so you replace a layer by playing over it.

Blur · Damp

Blur opens the feedback from each line feeding itself to a full cross-mix between all four, smearing the taps together. Damp rolls the high end off the tail.

Mix

The wet level of the whole Drift layer blended back into the master. A parallel ambient wash under the loops, not a replacement for them.

Sessions

Save the whole rig

Sixty-four slots on the ≡ (Menu) button. A session stores every setting, the punch-pad values, the FX-sequencer pattern and all recorded audio. Del (K4) erases a slot. Saving over a used slot, or deleting one, asks first; each is date-and-time named, and the header shows which session is loaded. All disk work happens on a background thread, never on the audio callback, so saving never glitches playback. Sessions live under /data/UserData/schwung/loopbox-sessions/ and survive reinstalls.
